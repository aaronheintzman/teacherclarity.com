Our Mission & Approach

Teacher clarity is being explicit about what students are learning, why it matters, and what success looks like. It is one of the most researched practices in education, and we help schools put it into consistent practice.

Our Mission

To make teacher clarity the standard in every classroom, not the exception.

Our Vision

A future where every teacher has the tools, coaching, and support to be crystal clear in their craft, and where that clarity becomes the foundation of equitable student achievement.

Why Teacher Clarity

A Proven Idea, Applied Consistently

Teacher clarity isn't new. It first emerged as a formal concept in education research in the early 1970s, and John Hattie's Visible Learning® synthesis places it among the practices with the strongest evidence for student learning.

The challenge has never been knowing that clarity matters. It's making it consistent across classrooms, grade levels, and schools: clear learning intentions, success criteria, and feedback in every lesson.

Teacher Clarity is the instructional framework and professional learning offering of Clarity in School, the consulting organization behind this site. It exists to close the gap between research and daily practice, through coaching, professional learning, and leadership support. Clarity in School is led by Aaron Heintzman. What Guides Us

Our Core Values

These aren't words on a wall: they're the commitments that shape every client relationship, every workshop, and every coaching conversation we have.

Clarity

We believe clear learning intentions and success criteria are the foundation of every great lesson, and every great consulting engagement. We practice what we preach.

Evidence

Everything we do is grounded in research: Hattie's Visible Learning®, learning science, and the evidence base on effective coaching. We don't chase trends; we follow the data.

Partnership

We don't consult at schools: we partner with them. Every engagement is built on mutual respect, shared ownership, and a genuine belief in the educators we work with.

Impact

Good intentions aren't enough. We measure our work against real outcomes: for teachers, for leaders, and most importantly, for the students in their classrooms.
